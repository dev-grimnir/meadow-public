# Cedar-Slope Breeders

Species that complete a **full breeding cycle on the cedar (west) slopes** — egg → larva →
pupa → adult — with every leg of the cycle supplied here: larval food (a host plant) or a
nest substrate, a place to develop and pupate, and an overwintering refuge. If the loop
closes without the animal leaving the slopes, it's a breeder. Anything that only forages
here — hummingbirds, goldfinches, honeybees, transient butterflies — lives in the companion
`feeders.md`.

**Scope:** the cedar slopes only. Broadway/Dogwood plants (purple coneflower) and the
natural-area ironweed are out of scope, so their insects aren't listed here.

The **overwinters** note is why the standing rules exist — *no fall cleanup, no spring
cutback in butterfly territory*: it's where these species spend the winter, on the stems and
in the litter.

## By host plant

### Milkweed — Common Milkweed + Butterfly Weed (north half)

- **Monarch** (*Danaus plexippus*) — the reason for the milkweed block. Breeds here through
  the summer (several generations); overwinters **off-site** in Mexico. Loop:
  *breeds-but-migrates* — no monarch overwinters where it breeds. Also a fall nectar visitor
  (see `feeders.md`).
- **Milkweed Tussock Moth** (*Euchaetes egle*) — larvae feed only on milkweed; pupate in the
  leaf litter. ⚠ **Manage down:** the caterpillars feed in swarms and can strip a milkweed
  plant bare — milkweed the monarchs need. Pull the larval clusters when they threaten the
  block.
- **Unexpected Cycnia** (*Cycnia inopinatus*) — milkweed specialist, favors Butterfly Weed;
  pupates in litter.

### Golden Alexanders (*Zizia aurea*) — north front

- **Black Swallowtail** (*Papilio polyxenes*) — Apiaceae host. Overwinters as a **chrysalis on
  the standing stems** — loop *closed* on-site.

### Asters — New England + Smooth Blue

- **Pearl Crescent** (*Phyciodes tharos*) — aster specialist.
- **Silvery Checkerspot** (*Chlosyne nycteis*).
- **Wavy-lined Emerald** (*Synchlora aerata*) — decorates itself with aster florets.
- All overwinter as **part-grown larvae in the leaf litter** — loop *closed*.

### Black-Eyed Susan (*Rudbeckia fulgida* var. *sullivantii*) — south mid-high

- **Silvery Checkerspot** (shared with the asters).
- **Gorgone Checkerspot** (*Chlosyne gorgone*) — *range-marginal here; may not occur.*
- **Wavy-lined Emerald.**
- ***Schinia* flower moths** — develop in the seed heads.

### Bee balm / bergamot (*Monarda didyma* / *M. fistulosa*) — south

- **Hermit Sphinx** (*Sphinx eremitus*) — Monarda / mint host.
- **Raspberry Pyrausta** (*Pyrausta signatalis*) and **Orange Mint Moth** (*P. orphisalis*).
- ***Dufourea monardae*** — a **Monarda-specialist native bee**: ground-nests on the slope and
  provisions its brood only from these flowers. The spared bergamot clumps keep its host
  present. Loop *closed*.

### Mountain Mint (*Pycnanthemum virginianum*) — north

- **Orange Mint Moth** (*Pyrausta orphisalis*) — shared with the bee balm. Otherwise mountain
  mint is mostly forage (→ `feeders.md`).

### Wild Columbine (*Aquilegia canadensis*) — south, walnut corner

- **Columbine Duskywing** (*Erynnis lucilius*) — columbine-specialist skipper; overwinters as
  a **larva**. Loop *closed*.

### Blazing Star (*Liatris spicata*) — south

- **Liatris Borer** (*Papaipema beeriana*) — *rare specialist.*
- **Bleeding Flower Moth** (*Schinia sanguinea*) — Liatris flower / seed feeder.

## Native bees — breed in the habitat itself

These don't hang on one plant; they nest on the slope and provision from the whole bloom.
The full cycle — nest, brood, overwinter — is on-site.

- **Ground nesters** in the bare, occulted soil: mining bees (*Andrena* — several
  aster / spring specialists), cellophane bees (*Colletes*), long-horned bees (*Melissodes*,
  composite specialists).
- **Stem nesters** in the standing dead stems (part of why they're left up): small carpenter
  bees (*Ceratina*), leafcutter bees (*Megachile*), mason bees (*Osmia*).
- **Bumblebees** (*Bombus*) — nest in ground / tussock cover, the queen overwinters in the
  litter; the colony completes its cycle on-site. Site nests away from the kids' clearing —
  they'll defend a disturbed nest.

## Not breeders (→ `feeders.md`)

Present on the slopes but they complete their life cycle elsewhere, so they're feeders:

- **Eastern Tiger Swallowtail** — hosts on the property's trees (wild cherry, tulip poplar,
  ash), not our plantings.
- **Honeybees** — managed livestock.
- **Hummingbirds, goldfinches** — nest off-site and range far past the slopes.
- **Painted Lady** and the other *Vanessa* ladies — migrants that don't overwinter here (no
  host in the palette anyway).
- **Sweat bees** (*Lasioglossum* / *Halictus*) — omitted.

**White Yarrow** is forage-only → `feeders.md`.
